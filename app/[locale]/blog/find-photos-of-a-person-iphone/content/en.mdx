**Quick answer:** To find every photo of a specific person on iPhone, use a face-grouping app that scans your library and sorts it by face. [FaceIdent](/faceident) does this entirely on-device — it detects faces, groups them into people, and lets you open one person to see all of their photos in seconds. ## The fastest way: group your library by face

Modern iPhones are fast enough to recognize faces without any server. A face-grouping app looks at each photo, finds the faces, and clusters the ones that belong to the same person. Instead of a flat timeline, you get a grid of people — tap a face and you see every shot they are in.

[FaceIdent](/faceident) is built around this idea and keeps the whole process on your device.

## Step-by-step: find every photo of a person

1. **Install a face-grouping app.** Download [FaceIdent](/faceident) from the App Store and allow access to your photos. It is free to try.
2. **Let it scan on-device.** The app detects faces and groups them into people right on your iPhone. Larger libraries take a little longer the first time, then stay fast.
3. **Open a person.** Tap any face group to see every photo that person appears in.
4. **Name the group.** Give the person a name so you can find them instantly next time.
5. **Tidy up.** If the same person is split into two groups, merge them. If two people are mixed together, move the wrong photos out — the app remembers your fix.

## Is on-device face grouping private?

Yes — when the recognition runs locally, the photos and the math that represents each face stay on the device. [FaceIdent](/faceident) has no cloud and no account: it never uploads your library, so grouping people does not trade your privacy for convenience.

## Common questions

**Can I find all photos of one person on iPhone?**
Yes. Apple's Photos app has a People album, and apps like FaceIdent group your whole library by face so you can open one person and see all their photos.

**Does this upload my photos?**
No. FaceIdent runs entirely on-device, so nothing is sent to a server.

**Why is one person in two groups?**
Different angles or lighting can fool the model. Merge the groups and the app keeps your correction.
